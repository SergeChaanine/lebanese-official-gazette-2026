| Chapter Two: Performing the Functions Mandated to
the NACC by Law

Despite the structural challenges facing the Commission—most notably delays
in adopting its internal regulations and code of conduct, and the resulting
administrative and regulatory constraints—it continued to exercise its legal powers
within the limits of available resources. The Commission successfully ensured
the uninterrupted performance of its core functions without fully upholding the
implementation of applicable laws. This was particularly demonstrated through
the continued collection of asset and interest declarations from civil servants and
other individuals subject to declarations requirements, thereby strengthening the
principle of accountability and preserving one of the essential pillars of the anti-
corruption prevention framework.

In the same context, the Commission handled a growing number of complaints
during the reporting year regarding the non-implementation of the Right of Access
to Information Law, particularly cases in which certain public administrations and
institutions either refused or delayed the provision of information to requesters.
During this period, the Commission issued approximately 28 preliminary and final
decisions, reflecting the gradual activation of its quasi-judicial role in this domain.
These efforts serve to reinforce the right of access to information and promote
administrative transparency.

Chapter Two of this report provides a detailed overview of the progress made
in fulfilling the Commission's functions and legal mandates, highlighting both
the achievements realized and the objectives that could not be met during the
reporting period due to circumstances beyond the Commission's control. Chief
