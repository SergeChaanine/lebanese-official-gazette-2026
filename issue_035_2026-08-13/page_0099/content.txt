National Anti-Corruption Commission

Annual Report 2025

| Foreword

We are pleased to present the Fourth Annual Report
of the National Anti-Corruption Commission (NACC),
covering the period from 1 January to 31 December
2025 Thisreportprovidesacomprehensive overview
of the NACC's activities and highlights the progress
achieved in carrying out its mandate in accordance
with the applicable laws and regulations

We issue this report in line with the principle
of transparency that underpins the work of the
Commission, and in compliance with the obligations
set forth in the Right of Access to Information Law
and the NACC's founding statute, in particular Article 24 Under this article, the NACC
is required to issue “annual reports on its activities. At a minimum, these reports
must include information on operating mechanisms (including costs, objectives,
rules, achievements, and any challenges encountered), audited accounts, general
policies adopted, projects implemented and those not implemented (with the
reasons for non-implementation), as well as any recommendations aimed at
enhancing and advancing its work The NACC must submit this report to the offices
of the President of the Republic, the Speaker of the Parliament, the Prime Minister,
as well as to deputies and ministers, and publish a copy in the Official Gazette and
on its official website “.

The year 2025 marked significant progress for the NACC across multiple fronts,
particularly in fulfilling the functions entrusted to it by law and expanding the
scope of its activities. As is customary at the beginning of each year, the NACC
issued a circular to civil servants subject to assets and interests declaration
requirements, calling upon them to submit their periodic declarations, as well
as initial declarations upon appointment, extension, or renewal of mandate, and
final declarations upon exit from office, within a two-month period At the time of
drafting this report, a total of 19,189 declarations had been submitted.

During the past year, the NACC continued to review and adjudicate complaints it
has received over time concerning non-compliance with the Right of Access to
Information Law. In this regard, the Commission issued 28 decisions relating to
these complaints

The Department of Recovery of Assets Derived from Corruption Crimes engaged
with national and international stakeholders to develop a comprehensive asset
recovery strategy, including through cooperation with the Stolen Asset Recovery
