| Introduction
The current context and the role of the Commission

In 2024, Lebanon scored 22 points on the Corruption Perceptions Index, marking a
decline from its 2023 score. Its global ranking also dropped from 149th to 154th out
of 180 countries. This decline reflects the persistent structural challenges within
Lebanon's integrity and governance framework It also underscores the urgent
need for coherent institutional interventions that move beyond temporary or
partial measures to address corruption.

In this context, 2025 marked a significant step forward in the work of the NACC, with
the launch of the first national assessment of corruption risks in the public sector,
carried out within the framework of its legally mandated powers The assessment
was developed and shared with public administrations and institutions and
was based on a comprehensive analytical approach covering several key areas:
the legal and regulatory framework; administrative procedures and decision-
making processes; the management of public expenditures and revenues; public
procurement; human resource management; information security, disclosure, and
access; ethics and professional conduct; whistleblowing mechanisms; corruption
reporting; grievance and appeals procedures; and anti-corruption policies. This
methodology enables the systematic and measurable identification of both
vulnerabilities and strengths.

At the same time, throughout 2025, the Commission continued implementing the
measures outlined in the action plan it launched in 2023, with the aim of translating
its commitments into practical and sustainable actions. As part of its mandate
to promote a culture of integrity and prevent corruption, particularly through
engagement with the education sector, the NACC signed several memoranda of
understanding with the American University of Beirut, the Lebanese American
University, and the Center for Educational Research and Development. These
partnerships aim to develop educational and awareness initiatives, including a
key component focused on introducing a mandatory anti-corruption course in the
faculties of the participating universities

In terms of institutional capacity-building, the NACC, in partnership with UNDP,
conducted a specialized training program delivered in three phases, aimed at
strengthening the capacities of its members to detect and preventillicit enrichment.
The program enabled participants to develop their skills in auditing asset and
interest declarations, investigating cases of illicit enrichment, and developing
operational procedures and electronic systems in line with international best
practices. The training also adopted a practical, hands-on approach, incorporating
case studies and interactive exercises.

As part of its efforts to strengthen administrative transparency, the NACC, with the
support of UNDP, is developing a dedicated digital platform to assess the extent to
which public administrations comply with the Right of Access to Information Law.
