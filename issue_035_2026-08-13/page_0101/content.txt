| Executive Summary

The year 2025 marked a turning point in the work of the NACC During this period,
the Commission moved beyond the establishment and gradual operationalization
phase that had characterized previous years and entered a more advanced stage
of institutional development. This progress enabled it to expand the exercise of its
legal powers and strengthen its impact on the national integrity system.

Following the adoption of its internal and financial regulations, organizational
structure, and code of conduct, and after completing the setup of its headquarters
and specialized directorates, the Commission became capable of carrying out its
mandate within a more stable institutional framework, despite ongoing challenges
related to human and financial resources. During 2025, the Commission continued
to perform its legally mandated duties, particularly under Law No. 175/2020 on
Combating Corruption in the Public Sector and the Establishment of the National
Anti-Corruption Commission, the Law on Assets and Interests Declarations and the
Punishment of Illicit Enrichment, and the Right of Access to Information Law

In this context, the NACC issued a circular to civil servants subject to assets and
interests declaration requirements, calling upon them to submit their periodic
declarations, as well as initial declarations upon appointment, extension, or
renewal of mandate, and final declarations at the end of their term, within the
legally prescribed deadlines At the time of drafting this report, a total of 19,189
declarations had been submitted, reflecting à growing level of compliance with
this legal obligation and placing increasing responsibility on the NACC to ensure
their review and follow-up

The NACC also placed particular emphasis on strengthening its oversight role in
ensuring the proper implementation of the Right of Access to Information Law. In
2025, it reviewed 3 number of complaints submitted to it and issued 28 decisions,
thereby contributing to the consolidation of this constitutional right and reinforcing
accountability across public administrations and institutions.

With regard to combating complex and transnational forms of corruption, the
Department of Recovery of Assets Derived from Corruption Crimes continued its
engagement with relevant stakeholders at both the national and international
levels, in cooperation with the Stolen Asset Recovery (StAR) Initiative, with the
aim of developing a comprehensive strategy for the recovery of stolen assets. In
addition, the NACC finalized a report assessing corruption risks within the public
sector and established a specialized committee to oversee the implementation
of the Whistleblower Protection Law, thereby strengthening the protection of
whistleblowers and reducing impunity. At the international level, the NACC also
led Lebanon's delegation to this year's Conference of the States Parties to the
United Nations Convention against Corruption, reaffirming its role as the national
focal point for the fulfilment of Lebanon's international commitments.
